# Voice and Register Guidance

Build a voice profile from observed language, relationships, and presentation
signals before translating a large project.

## Use evidence, not archetypes

Treat identity labels as hypotheses:

- A noble may be controlled and formally polite without speaking archaic
  Chinese.
- A wanderer may be blunt or fragmented without receiving a Chinese regional
  dialect.
- A deity may sound detached, repetitive, intimate, or alien rather than
  automatically using `吾` and `汝`.

Do not add a Chinese regional identity, social stereotype, or historical era
that the project does not support.

## Describe observable dimensions

Record:

- formality and politeness
- sentence length, completeness, and rhythm
- directness, hedging, commands, and questions
- address terms by relationship
- preferred vocabulary and recurring constructions
- humor, sarcasm, profanity, and emotional intensity
- pronoun and subject omission
- punctuation, pauses, repetitions, and verbal fillers
- public/private, calm/emotional, disguised, possessed, or transformed modes

Model relationship-dependent variants instead of assuming one immutable voice
per speaker.

## Interpret typography cautiously

Fonts, colors, and styles can mark a speaker, narrator, thought, deity, AI,
ghost, broadcast, letter, dream, timeline, emotion, or mere decoration.

Before assigning a translation register:

1. Locate every repeated use of the visual marker.
2. Check Character definitions, text tags, styles, and surrounding scenes.
3. Determine whether one marker maps to several voices or one voice uses
   several markers.
4. Record the mapping as evidence with confidence, not as a fact inferred from
   appearance alone.

## Profile with bounded samples

- Sample across chapters, interlocutors, emotions, and style variants.
- Prefer 12–20 scene samples for major voices, 5–8 for recurring secondary
  voices, and 2–3 for minor voices as starting limits.
- Include only enough neighboring lines to recover intent.
- Add samples only when evidence conflicts or confidence remains low.
- Calibrate high-impact voices on a short scene before batch translation.

For a continuing game, reuse the approved profile and analyze only new or
changed evidence. Do not rediscover stable voices on every release.

## Approval boundary

Ask for confirmation when a choice materially shapes the whole project:

- name and address systems
- dialect or archaic-language policy
- narrator literary level
- social-register contrasts
- divine, nonhuman, or typographically marked voices

Mark decisions as `approved`, `provisional`, or `review`. Report later evidence
that conflicts with an approved decision instead of silently drifting.
